Hypotheses
• Symptoms confined to one bad node: contradicted. This would require the errors to come from one node, but they come from 512 nodes [r_76b8079e.f12]. A single shared component could still be the cause; that stays open.
• The peer at 172.16.96.116 drops or closes the control streams: supported. Every matching line names that peer.
  ◦ Background: this is probably the service or front-end node running the job-control side of ciod.
  ◦ Whether it failed, or closed the streams deliberately because a job aborted, is not established.
• Job launch fails because a working directory is missing, and ciod streams are then torn down: supported, as an association only. It rests on the precursor seen before 6 of 7 episodes.
  ◦ Background: the LOGIN chdir error usually means the job’s working directory doesn’t exist on the I/O nodes’ filesystem. That could be a bad user path or a missing NFS/GPFS mount.

Next check
• Check what the LOGIN chdir errors share. Search LOGIN chdir around each episode start and compare the <PATH> values and nodes.
  ◦ Same path every time: points to one user or job config. That supports the job-side explanation over a peer fault.
  ◦ Different paths, or all I/O nodes failing together: points to a filesystem mount problem on the I/O nodes.
  ◦ Either way, the logs can’t rule out the peer host as the root cause.
• Look at the one episode without the chdir precursor. Open its start with get_log_context at dataset scope and look for MMCS or job-kill events. Seeing a different precursor there doesn’t prove a different cause.
• Get records from outside these logs. Scheduler and job records for those timestamps, and syslog from 172.16.96.116, are what would actually tell you whether a job aborted or the peer failed.
