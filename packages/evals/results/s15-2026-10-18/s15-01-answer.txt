Narrowing
I looked for what shows up around each burst. Across the 7 bursts [r_4a025704.f1], ciod: LOGIN chdir(<PATH>) failed: No such file or directory appeared in the 10 minutes before 6 of them [r_4a025704.f6]. That is about 145.2× [r_4a025704.f9] more often than its normal rate. It also appeared after 3 bursts [r_4a025704.f17]. The DDR/EDRAM corrected-error messages near the bursts are close to their usual rate, so they don’t look related.

Hypotheses
- A single failing node or rack is the cause: contradicted. The breadth and even spread above rule this out.
- The ciod peer at 172.16.96.116 dropped or reset the control connections: supported. Every event names that address, and many nodes fail within the same second. Supported isn’t proven, though: the logs show the symptom, not why the peer went away.
- A job launch with a bad working directory triggers the bursts: supported. The chdir failure precedes most bursts. This is timing only, not proof. As background, a failed login/chdir would make ciod abort the job, and the I/O nodes would then see their control sockets close. That would fit, but the logs don’t confirm it.

Next check
- Precursor lines: for each burst, pull the ciod: LOGIN chdir(...) lines and see which path they name. If it’s the same path or user each time, a misconfigured job or a missing filesystem mount is the likely trigger.
- Front-end logs: check the host behind 172.16.96.116 (service node or job launcher) at those burst times for a job kill, restart or network drop. A reset there would confirm the peer theory. If that host was clean, look at the network path between the I/O nodes and it.
- The odd burst: the 2005-06-09 episode is much smaller than the others. If it wasn’t preceded by the chdir message, it may have a different trigger.
