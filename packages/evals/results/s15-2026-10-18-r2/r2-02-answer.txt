Each burst is usually preceded by a ciod LOGIN chdir failure. Your next step is to check the job’s working directory and whether the shared filesystem was reachable at those burst times.

Narrowing
The local-versus-spread test points to spread. Every rack is affected, the per-node counts are small, and the timing is simultaneous. As background: in BG/L, ciod on each I/O node keeps a control stream to the job-control/mpirun front end. When every I/O node loses that stream at once, the likely cause is the peer or job side, not the I/O nodes.

- Job launch or working-directory failure triggers a job-wide teardown: supported, not proven. A precursor check showed that “ciod: LOGIN chdir(<PATH>) failed: No such file or directory” appeared in the 10 minutes before 6 [r_7b6f03e4.f6] of 7 [r_7b6f03e4.f1] episode starts. - Failing control peer or network path to 172.16.96.116: open. It fits the single shared peer, but I haven’t run a check that separates it from the hypothesis above.
- One bad node: contradicted by the node/rack breadth and the synchronized timing.

Next check

- Then look at the one burst without it. Find which of the episodes had no chdir precursor and check its neighbourhood for front-end or MMCS events. If you find control-plane or network errors there, the peer/network hypothesis gets stronger.
